# Kikroo trademark territory and cost strategy

**Decision date:** 2026-08-17
**Status:** planning record, not legal advice and not authorization to file.

## Recommended route

1. Confirm the exact Brazilian company applicant and its entitlement to any INPI discount.
2. Complete live professional similarity searches for `KIKROO` and related phonetic/image variants.
3. If cleared, file the **KIKROO word mark in Brazil first**, using professionally approved goods/services wording. Classes 9 and 42 remain candidates, not a final selection.
4. Record the Brazilian filing date. The Paris Convention normally gives a six-month priority window for later filings of the same mark and covered goods/services.
5. During those six months, use beta evidence to choose real target markets. Compare direct national/regional filing with a Madrid application; do not designate countries merely because they are available.
6. Evaluate the combined rooster/globe/clock logo separately after its source, human contribution, similarity risk, and final artwork are documented.

## Direct filing versus Madrid

### Direct filings make more sense when

- only one important foreign market is being pursued;
- local filing/use rules need a tailored strategy;
- independence from the Brazilian base application is valuable;
- local counsel is already required for clearance or commercial reasons.

### Madrid makes more sense when

- several member territories are selected at the same time;
- the mark and goods/services match the Brazilian base;
- centralized renewal and later territorial designations reduce administration;
- the company accepts the five-year dependency on the base mark.

Madrid is not a worldwide registration. It is a managed bundle of territorial requests. A designated office may refuse protection, and responding can require local counsel. For five years, loss or restriction of the Brazilian base can affect the international registration to the same extent (the central-dependency risk).

## Budget gate for Kikroo

Do not begin international filing simply because the product is intended for global use. Approve a territory only when at least one of these exists:

- meaningful tester/user evidence there;
- an imminent store or commercial launch;
- a partner, customer, or marketing commitment;
- a credible conflicting-use risk that justifies the expense.

The provisional 24-month review list is Brazil first, followed by the European Union and United States only if beta/store evidence supports them. This is a planning shortlist, not an approved filing instruction.
